Why smoothies

We are university students with limited time, and we thought about what every one of us consumes every day: food and beverage.

So we ran a concise campus sample, at CUHK, and asked the campus what a drink stand should be.

The gap

Competing campus beverage services such as Cafe Tolo, CC Kunkle and UC Boba are widely used — but the most popular options are non-specialist canteen drinks and vending machines. Specialist drinks are not making enough impact.

CUE fills that gap: a specialist smoothie that fits the rise in health consciousness and student budgets.

The challenge: canteen drinks are cheap add-ons to meals and always convenient. So CUE must be fairly priced for its quality, and highly convenient in place and time.

How we found out

raw responses

First an exploratory interview: a student who buys a drink at least once a day (fruit tea) walked us through habits, priorities and pain points — health and queueing — and what they think of smoothies.

Then a conclusive descriptive survey, designed from that interview and sent to peers: raw responses, usable after cleaning.

The main keywords in the free answers were price, location and convenience and taste — with health a major concern.

The gap between the two medians is the whole pricing problem, and the survey says there is room: would pay more than their usual spend for a smoothie.

At the cart: bring your CUHK student ID for the lowest base price of HK$25 for a Regular; walk-ins pay HK$28. Each size step adds to that (Large and XL, ). The first extra is included, further extras are HK$5 each.

Promotion

The CUHK Buddy Bundle: buy 2, get 10 percent off. It is aimed at the two biggest occasions — hanging out with friends () and the afternoon cool-down window (), which is why the bundle runs 14:00–17:00.

A mobile web pre-ordering app: “Skip the queue, grab your CUE” — of respondents agreed they would use it.

On the ground: Instagram promos, and a modern lifestyle-cafe pickup vibe with smooth R&B playing near YIA.

Our test

Before committing, we will A/B test the atmosphere at the YIA seating area for two weeks, 14:00–17:00:

  • Control: cozy tables with campus ambient noise.
  • Treatment: the same, plus a light instrumental / R&B playlist at a low, study-friendly volume.

The menu, prices, bundle and staffing stay constant. We measure average dwell time, seat-conversion rate and repeat visits and add-on rate.

What we would do better

Align the survey with the interview. We would add the texture and heaviness of the drink, and how hard it is to find a healthy drink on campus — both came up in the interview but not in the questionnaire.

Sample better. Almost half of the respondents were year 3 students. With near-stratified sampling we would reach year 1 and 2 as well, and read the campus as a whole.

Where that leaves us

  • Lead the ads with taste, then freshness, less sugar, and online ordering.
  • Set up at YIA: it got the most votes () and is nearer the MTR station.
  • Two thirds of respondents have never bought a smoothie — and most would buy on campus if the price is fair and the quality is good.
