# CUE Smoothie: One-Page Summary
*MKTG3010 group project · Blended fresh on your cue.*

**The idea.** CUE is a fresh-fruit smoothie cart for CUHK students. It starts as one cart on the ground floor of the YIA Building, next to the stairwell. It is built from what students told us in two interviews and a survey, and its look is a vintage "Fruit Ticket" theme.

**Market gap.** Students already buy drinks at CUHK, but mostly non-specialist ones: canteen drinks (78%) and vending machines (61%). They usually buy them as a cheap add-on to a meal, at a median spend of about HK$10. Specialist outlets (Café Tolo, CC Kunkle, UC Boba) make little impact, and none sells smoothies or fresh fruit. The old YIA café sold smoothies at about HK$25 and closed. CUE can fill this gap with a drink that is healthier and priced for student budgets.

**Customer and method.** We ran 2 exploratory interviews, then a 37-question Google Form survey. The survey went through eligibility, drink habits, six importance factors, agreement statements with an attention check, menu choices, price and intent, open text, location and demographics. After cleaning, 55 of 72 responses were kept (the pitch text says 53; see the note below). About half were Year 3 students. Taste ranked first among buying factors (28%), then price (22%) and healthiness (14%). About 69% said they probably or definitely would buy, and about two-thirds had never bought a smoothie, so the product is new to most of them.

**STP.** *Segments:* three groups, all led by taste. Two are "taste + price" and one is "taste + freshness". The freshness group has the highest intent (79% top-2-box). *Target:* CUHK undergraduates who are health-conscious and on a budget, buying with friends or as an afternoon treat. *Positioning:* a modern, aspirational lifestyle drink (fresh, tasty, fairly priced) that sits between a vending-machine can and an off-campus smoothie chain.

**Product and place.**
- **Menu.** Three collections: *CUE Layers* (Mango Sunrise, Berry Dusk, Peach Cloud), *Fruit Classics* (Berry Blush, Banana Boost, Peach Breeze, Blue Moon) and *Morning Blends* (Matcha Banana, Coffee Oat, Berry Oat). A *Mix Your Own* option lets students pick one or two fruits, a base and add-ons. Mango (72%) and strawberry (60%) were the top fruits, so both feature in Mango Sunrise.
- **Packaging.** Clear cups in three sizes (16, 20 and 24 oz), because 74% wanted a choice of sizes. Striped straws, vintage labels and kraft paper takeaway bags carry the Fruit Ticket identity.
- **Place.** YIA (47%) and the Central Campus teaching buildings (47%) tied as the preferred locations. We chose YIA because it is closer to the MTR station and gets more passing traffic. A second Central Campus cart would be tested later, based on sales.

**Price.**
- **Evidence.** The median willingness to pay is HK$30, against a usual spend of HK$10. 92% would pay above their usual spend. HK$25 gives the best revenue index and is about 45–55% cheaper than off-campus smoothie chains.
- **Menu.** Regular (16 oz, 1 add-on) is HK$25 with a CUHK student ID and HK$28 for walk-ins. Upgrades are about +HK$5 per cup size, and extra add-ons are +HK$5 each.

**Promotion.**
1. **Buddy Bundle (buy 2, get 10% off).** It targets "hanging out with friends" (53%) and afternoon treats (49%) from 14:00 to 17:00.
2. **Mobile web pre-order page.** 64% agreed they would use it. It cuts queues at the lunch and afternoon peaks and checks student ID for the HK$25 price.
3. **Lifestyle branding.** Instagram promos plus a smooth R&B and pop atmosphere at the YIA pickup spot.
4. **A/B test (2 weeks).** Seating with no music versus seating with a light R&B playlist, 14:00–17:00. We measure dwell time, seat-conversion rate, repeat visits and add-on rate.

**Conclusion.**
- Lead ads with taste, then show freshness, less artificial sugar and online ordering to skip queues.
- Open at YIA and expand only if sales support it.
- Most respondents said they would buy on campus if the price is fair and the quality good.

**Limitations.**
- The sample skews to Year 3 students (about 50%). Next time, use near-stratified convenience sampling to reach Years 1 and 2.
- Align survey questions with the interview themes, for example texture and heaviness, and how hard it is to find a healthy drink on campus.
